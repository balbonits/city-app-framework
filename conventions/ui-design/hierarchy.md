# Hierarchy & Typography

Hierarchy is created by contrast across three levers: **size, position, color**. If everything is the same, nothing is important.

---

## The three levers

- **Size.** Bigger = more important. Headline >> body >> subtext.
- **Position.** Higher = more important. Most important content sits at the top.
- **Color.** More saturated, darker, or accent-colored = more important. Subdued = secondary.

You don't need all three. One strong lever beats three weak ones. But a card that uses none reads as a spreadsheet.

---

## Applying it

- Most important info: top, larger, colored or bold.
- Use images aggressively for scannability — image + text card scans 5× faster than text-only.
- Replace "from / to" labels with icons + lines where possible (visual > textual).
- Right-align price/cost (the eye expects it on the right).

---

## Type scale

- **Landing pages:** ≤ 6 font sizes. Wide range (12px → 64px+).
- **Dashboards / product UI:** Rarely larger than 24px. Info density punishes large headers.
- **One sans-serif is enough.** Don't fixate on font picking. Inter, Geist, SF Pro, Helvetica — pick and move on.

---

## The tightening hack

For headers and large text, default browser settings look loose. Tighten:

- **Letter-spacing:** -2% to -3%
- **Line-height:** 110–120% (default is ~140%, too loose at large sizes)

This is the single highest-leverage change for making large text feel professional.

---

## Body text

- Don't tighten body text the same way. Default line-height (140–160%) is right for reading.
- Body should hit 15–20% white (see [color.md](color.md)). Pure black on pure white is harsh.
